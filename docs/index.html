Quickstart Why it exists

Two halves, one app

Most tools give you one or the other.

Chat interfaces talk to models and know nothing about your agent. Evaluation platforms are dashboards for engineers, and your conversations live on someone else's servers. You end up running both, and neither knows about the other.

The chat half

  • Replies stream in; stop mid-answer and keep what was written
  • Attach files, paste screenshots straight from the clipboard
  • Thumbs with a comment that stays under the reply for good
  • Share a link to a conversation — or one exact turn
  • Bring your own model key; it never leaves the browser

The studio half

  • Every instruction save is a new version — diff and roll back
  • Replay real conversations under a changed version or model
  • Compare side by side as results fill in live
  • Score with an LLM judge and read its reasoning
  • Open any turn's trace: agent → tool → LLM, with cost

The loop

Prove the change was an improvement.

  1. Talk to the agent

    Real conversations accumulate — from you, your users, or a headless client posting through the same public API.

  2. Change the instructions

    Every save writes a new version. Nothing is ever overwritten, so v14 is still there when v15 disappoints.

  3. Replay yesterday's conversations against it

    Each turn re-runs under the context it originally recorded — same date, same timezone — so the only thing that varies is your change.

  4. Read the verdict

    Baseline beside each candidate, filling in live. Score with a judge and read its reasoning. Every past score is kept.

Bring your own backend

It does not want to be your agent framework.

Build the agent however you like — ADK, LangGraph, Mastra, PydanticAI, your own. Point Cupel at it, and check what is missing before you start:

# see how much of the contract your backend already speaks
npx cupel-ready https://api.example.com/openapi.json
→ 44/69 operations · missing: eval workbench, memory, casebooks

# generate the config for it — base URL, route prefix, auth scheme
npx cupel-ready https://api.example.com/openapi.json --init

No backend yet? It ships with one. The bundled server implements the contract and seeds itself with realistic conversations, so the whole app works before you have written a line of backend code.

Deliberately not

What it will never do.

  • Build your agent. There are good frameworks for that. This drives the one you have.
  • Host your model. Your keys, your provider, your bill.
  • Take your data. There is no cloud to send it to. Conversations stay where you run it.
  • Hold features back. No paid tier, no enterprise edition, no seat count.

Why “cupel”. A cupel is the shallow bone-ash dish used to assay silver. Molten metal is held in it under heat: the lead and impurities are absorbed into the dish, and what is actually valuable stays behind. It seemed like the right name for a tool whose job is telling you whether the change you just made was worth keeping.
